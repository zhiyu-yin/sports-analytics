# Injury-warning labeling specification — Phase 1 draft

Status: feasibility and selected-case audit only. No model fitted, split selected, or final holdout opened. This protocol is not a modeling freeze. Predictive performance would not demonstrate preventability or intervention benefit.

## Estimand and population

After each completed NBA regular-season game, estimate whether an eligible currently available player sustains a new injury in **(prediction_at, prediction_at + 14 elapsed days]**, with subsequent severity evaluated separately as (1) strictly more than 28 consecutive days of injury-related unavailability and (2) at least 10 consecutive injury-related missed team games. A timestamp is not a calendar game date. The completion time and availability time of every required input must be established; otherwise the timestamp is not production eligible.

Build the full eligible player-time population independently of later injuries. The audit is deliberately selected and cannot supply the cohort denominator. A player must have positive, timestamped evidence of availability; current injury-unavailable players are excluded. Probable, questionable, doubtful, not-yet-submitted, absent from a box score, and absent from a report are not automatic evidence of health. Record unknown availability rather than selecting players by future returns. This conservative rule may produce poor cohort coverage: measure that before modeling. Rest-only and other noninjury statuses do not establish an injury episode; they also do not establish point-in-time availability.

An injury sustained in the game just completed precedes that prediction timestamp. It is not a future outcome for that row and may make the player ineligible. Earlier eligible timestamps can still precede that injury. Public symptoms define a separately reported symptom-informed task; do not call that presymptomatic warning.

## Evidence, time and duration

Keep onset, first public report, earliest reviewed report, first injury-related missed game, medical clearance, activity clearance, first return appearance and evidence retrieval time distinct. The first report we find is not necessarily the first public report. Article update dates are not original publication dates. Retrospective articles may support outcomes only; no current biography or later diagnosis enters a historical predictor.

Store exact instants with UTC offsets, normalize comparisons to instants, and retain the original timezone/precision. Date-only onset is a whole local-day interval, not midnight as an invented exact onset. Use the event venue timezone where established; otherwise preserve the date and mark timezone unknown. Unknown onset remains unknown. An interval wholly inside the future window can be positive; an interval straddling either boundary remains unresolved for window membership. The audit CSV retains dates rather than fabricating instants; it is intentionally not ready to join prediction rows.

For continuously evidenced injury-unavailability intervals with date-only endpoints, calendar duration is **local_date(end) minus local_date(start)**, using a start-inclusive, end-exclusive convention. Dates require a documented common timezone. If endpoints are intervals, compute conservative lower and upper bounds; never treat a date difference as precisely observed medical time. Strictly >28 means 29 or more complete calendar days under this date convention. An upper bound <=28 supports a negative severity label. A lower bound >28 supports a positive label even when the final return is right censored. A lower bound of 28 alone is unknown, not positive or negative.

The first injury-related missed game is not automatically injury onset or the start of medical unavailability. Count consecutive missed **team** games only with an injury reason for each game or a credible retrospective source explicitly identifying the consecutive injury-related absence. Trades require joining the correct team schedule at each date. Rest, illness, personal reasons, suspension, coaching decisions, or unresolved mixed reasons break certainty of an injury-only run. A source's explicit aggregate consecutive missed-game count can support the secondary outcome; do not reconstruct it from appearances alone.

The onset-to-first-return appearance gap is a named **proxy**, never an automatic positive primary outcome. Return can give a conservative upper bound for a short episode, but does not prove the player was medically unavailable for every preceding day. On-court activity clearance is not full competition clearance. Persistent injury sidelining explicitly documented across an active-season interval can support a primary positive without a clearance date. Long reevaluation forecasts and season-ending surgery announcements alone do not prove realized duration.

Do not extend intervals through the offseason using missing games. Within a documented active competition period, off-days and the All-Star break can count only where injury unavailability is continuously supported. Crossing an offseason needs independent medical evidence; otherwise censor at the end of verified active follow-up. Postseason evidence may establish severity of an injury following an eligible regular-season prediction, but postseason games never create prediction timestamps in this initial scope. Offseason onset inside a late-season 14-day window requires independent reporting; absent that coverage the window is unknown, not negative.

## Episode identity and uncertainty

Group reports only with an adjudicated episode ID and stable player ID. Same diagnosis, same limb or short separation is insufficient. Establish continuity using explicit recovery/continuation evidence. Mark a documented new injury as new, documented recurrence as recurrence, and unsupported distinctions as unresolved. Unresolved recurrence cannot silently become a new event. Mechanism is contact, non_contact, or unknown and requires explicit evidence; a diagnosis alone supplies none. All mechanisms remain unknown in the present audit.

Audit IDs identify candidate records, including exclusions and status-only leads; they are not validated NBA IDs. Player IDs are deliberately blank pending reviewed mapping. All candidate records have provisional automated review, not independent human adjudication. Preserve source links, page/paragraph locators, uncertainty flags, adjudicator and timestamp. Human review should accept, amend or reject each assertion separately.

## Labels and ascertainment

Both outcomes use positive / negative / unknown / excluded. Severity and future-window membership are separate steps. A selected candidate's severity label is not a positive prediction row. A complete-onset-surveillance flag is mandatory before assigning a negative cohort outcome; absence of an injury article is not complete surveillance. If follow-up is incomplete, onset is ambiguous, or any plausible in-window injury has unresolved severity, return unknown unless a definite qualifying event already establishes a positive.

For each outcome record label_known_at as the latest original public availability time of the evidence needed to establish onset, attribution and severity. It is neither onset time nor necessarily return time. When that availability time cannot be verified, the label cannot enter historical training at a claimed cutoff. A prospective 28-day forecast does not mature the label. Negative rows need complete 14-day onset surveillance and adequate severity resolution for every in-window candidate; the follow-up requirement can exceed 42 days when onset is uncertain or absence starts later.

## Point-in-time features and validation safeguards

Candidate features are age, position, NBA experience, prior injuries publicly known by t, minutes and appearances in trailing 7/14/28-day windows, workload changes, consecutive appearances, back-to-backs, rest days, and deviations from a player's past-only baseline. A game enters workload only when its completion and statistics publication are both <=t. The trailing interval is (t-k days,t]. No future-season totals, future absences, return dates, retrospective mechanism classifications or future baselines are allowed. Fit imputation, scaling and feature selection on training only. Tracking speed, jumps, drives and travel are deferred because reliable historical point-in-time availability was not established.

Use chronological train, validation and sealed final test periods once cohort quality is demonstrated. At every historical training cutoff require mature outcome evidence, prediction horizon strictly before the cutoff, and purge all training rows linked to episodes appearing in evaluation. With uncertain episode identity or windows spanning the cutoff, purge conservatively. Preprocessing must not be refit on validation/test. Existing game-prediction protected periods (2024-25 and later) remain inaccessible to this module; no existing experiment executor is imported or run.

Freeze the cohort definition, outcome definitions, exclusions, exact split dates, embargo/purging procedure, predictors, preprocessing, models, tuning limits, alert thresholds and alert-merging rule in a separate Git commit before requesting a one-time final holdout opening. This phase does not create or consume that authorization.
