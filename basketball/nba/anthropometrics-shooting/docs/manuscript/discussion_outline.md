# Discussion structure

## Principal finding

Greater residual wingspan was associated with lower free-throw accuracy in NBA players with available combine measurements. The negative association was observed in the 2013-14 development analysis and persisted in a 13-season replication panel excluding that season, with similar estimates across player-clustered, beta-binomial, first-season-only, player-balanced, attempt-threshold and leave-one-season-out analyses. Each additional inch of wingspan beyond that expected for height was associated with approximately 3% lower adjusted odds of a made free throw. Odds are not percentage points. This is a between-player association and does not represent changing one player's anatomy. [D01]

## Development and replication comparison

The negative association appeared in the 2013-14 development analyses and persisted across additional seasons. Development estimates used different populations and variance assumptions. The development-stage joint high-leverage removal changed magnitude materially; its frozen scientific classification was UNCERTAIN DIRECTION, driven by magnitude sensitivity rather than observed sign reversal. Retain that qualification in the supplement. Overlapping players prevent an independent-player replication claim. [D02]

## Plausible but untested interpretations

Shooting mechanics or release geometry could be hypotheses for future work, but neither was measured or established here. The observed association could also reflect role allocation, player selection, or unmeasured shooting skill. Avoid attributing the association to a particular biomechanical pathway. [D03]

## Role and selection confounding

Usable-measurement coverage ranged from 51.0% to 67.3% across the approved season series. Measured players are not a random sample of all NBA players. Combine participation, measurement availability and completeness, NBA survival, playing opportunity and shooting opportunity may select the sample; the model does not correct this selection. Unmeasured skill, training and current role remain possible sources of confounding. Original combine position is a historical proxy and may not represent current playing role. [D04]

## Informative opportunity

Free-throw attempt volume was used as the grouped-binomial denominator rather than as a predictor. Previous model-adequacy analyses indicated that attempt volume is informative about player-level shooting and opportunity structure. Consequently, the primary model should be interpreted as a working conditional association model rather than a complete generative model of shooting opportunity. Player-clustered uncertainty does not repair conditional-mean misspecification. The supplementary attempt-volume diagnostic makes the residual pattern visible without making model misfit the main result. [D05]

## Why causal mechanism is not established

Residual wingspan is time invariant, so its coefficient is identified between players. Repeated seasons provide additional outcome information and dependence-aware uncertainty, but not within-player exposure identification. Player clustering is not adjustment for player fixed effects or unmeasured player ability. Neither the adjusted coefficient nor repeated-measures uncertainty establishes what would happen if a player's anatomy were changed. There is no causal identification design or selection correction. [D06]

## Strengths

Strengths include direct source corroboration, fixed measurement and identity rules, a frozen outcome-independent geometric definition, count-based modeling, dependence-aware uncertainty, a season held out of replication because it was used for development, and transparent predeclared sensitivity results. Numerical reliability was evaluated separately from scientific adequacy. [D07]

## Limitations

The main limitations are selected measurement coverage, informative shooting opportunity and possible conditional-mean misspecification, and between-player confounding. Further limitations include measurement error, frozen reference-fit uncertainty, small original position categories, unbalanced careers, left-truncated NBA observation and limited applicability beyond the measured NBA sample. More elaborate hierarchical joint models of free-throw opportunity and accuracy were investigated during model development. These models showed posterior predictive misspecification or weak identification among opportunity and player-level latent effects, with predictive checks withheld when sampling failed. They were therefore not used for primary scientific inference. Their adequacy failures remain part of the scientific history. [D08]

## Implications and future biomechanics work

The results motivate targeted measurement of mechanics, training and contemporary role in future research. Such work would require a separately designed analysis. No additional exposure, threshold, subgroup, model or causal analysis is part of the current manuscript freeze. [D09]
