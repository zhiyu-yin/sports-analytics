# Structured abstract skeleton

## Background

Relative wingspan may be associated with free-throw accuracy, but player selection and heterogeneous shooting opportunity complicate interpretation. This study evaluates a prespecified adjusted association using previously validated measurements and count outcomes. [A01]

## Methods

The primary replication panel comprised 13 seasons, 4,117 player-seasons and 857 accounting players. Of these, 3,980 positive-FTA rows from 835 players contributed 469,099 attempts; 137 zero-FTA rows remained in accounting. The 2013-14 development season was excluded. A grouped-binomial logistic model adjusted for barefoot height, original combine position and season; player-cluster CR1 covariance and a predeclared 2,000-replicate player bootstrap quantified uncertainty. [A02]

## Results

The primary grouped-binomial estimate was beta=-0.03045199 per residual inch (OR=0.970007); the 95% player-cluster CR1 interval was [-0.05228116, -0.00862282] and the two-sided p-value was 0.00625365. The player-bootstrap percentile interval was [-0.05433056, -0.01047528]. Both intervals describe uncertainty around the same original primary point estimate. [A03]

The negative direction persisted across beta-binomial, first-season-only, player-balanced, attempt-threshold and leave-one-season-out analyses. Beta-binomial uncertainty uses a separately documented numerical remediation; the historical execution failure remains preserved. [A04]

## Conclusions

Greater residual wingspan was associated with lower free-throw accuracy in NBA players with available combine measurements. The negative association was observed in the 2013-14 development analysis and persisted in a 13-season replication panel excluding that season, with similar estimates across player-clustered, beta-binomial, first-season-only, player-balanced, attempt-threshold and leave-one-season-out analyses. [A05]

The result is a between-player association in a selected measured-player sample. Informative opportunity and unmeasured player differences limit interpretation; causal and biomechanical mechanisms are not established. [A06]
